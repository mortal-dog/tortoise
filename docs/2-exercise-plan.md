# 2 · Exercise plan

> **The goal:** at 99, stand with a straight back, jump, and run.
> **On the way:** a freestanding handstand, a front split and a side split.

This is the training plan the Tortoise app runs. It needs only what is already at home: one pair of fixed dumbbells
(7–15 kg each), a jump rope, and a wall, a chair, a sofa and the floor. On outdoor days, playground or park bars are used
for hanging and pulling. Nothing has to be bought. Running happens outdoors whenever the weather allows; the rope replaces
it indoors.

It is written for a healthy adult starting in their late 30s. It starts gently, with the Week 0 tests followed by a 3-week
ramp, and the tests set the starting level of every exercise.

**How to read the numbers.** Every number comes from a study or guideline in [sources.md](sources.md), named in square
brackets like `[lee2014]`. A number that is this plan's own choice is marked *our rule*.

**Not medical advice.** Read [§13 Safety](#13-safety) before starting.

## 1. The goals and what they need

| At 99, and on the way | What it needs | Where it lives in the week |
|---|---|---|
| **A straight back** | Strong back extensors, a mobile upper back, strong bones (a crushed spine bone tips the back forward) | Morning block every day; loaded back extension Monday; bone loading from lifts, jumps, rope and running |
| **Jump** | Leg power, springy tendons, strong bones, confident landings | Jumps Monday; rope in every warm-up; pogo and skater hops Thursday |
| **Run** | An aerobic base and peak aerobic power; feet, calves and tendons used to running; hamstrings that tolerate speed | Easy run Tuesday, intervals Thursday, long run Saturday; calf, shin and Nordic work |
| **Get up off the floor, carry, climb stairs, not fall** | Leg strength, grip, balance | Strength A–C, carries, daily balance |
| **Handstand** | Wrists that carry body weight, shoulder strength, full overhead range, a hollow body line, the balance skill | 6 minutes every morning; pike push-ups, overhead press, hollow holds, hangs |
| **Front split** | Long hamstrings (front leg) and hip flexors (back leg), and strength at that length | Front-split block 5 days a week; Romanian deadlifts and Nordic curls |
| **Side split** | Long, strong adductors (inner thighs), and hips shaped to allow it | Side-split block 5 days a week; Cossack squats and Copenhagen planks |

### Why a straight back needs strong bones, not only good posture

- **A hunched back is more than looks.** People with a hyperkyphotic posture died at a 1.44 times higher rate
  [kado2004]. The most hunched had 3.7 times the odds of needing their arms to rise from a chair [kado2005].
- **Posture work has to last a lifetime.** Spine strengthening with posture training cut the curve by 3.0° in 6 months,
  and 1.6° of it came back in the 6 months after training stopped [sheaf2017]. That is why the straight-back routine runs
  every morning rather than in a 12-week course.
- **Bone answers loads that come and go.** Bone responds to dynamic loading, such as lifting, lowering and landing, not to
  a weight held still [turner1998]. A constant load lost as much bone as no load at all [lanyon1984]. Walking alone did
  not change spine density [ma2013].
- **Heavier, for you, builds more.** Supervised heavy lifting with jumps raised spine density by 2.9% in women
  [liftmor2018] and 4.1% in men [harding2020]. In the same trial the women's upper-back curve fell 6.7°, against 1.6°
  in controls [liftmor2019]. Leg resistance training raised hip density [howe2011], and in men exercise had small,
  positive effects on bone [mages2021]. At home, without supervision, the plan stays at "heavy for you" (8–12 clean
  reps) [ros2022], made heavier by one-leg variants, slower tempo and backpack load.

### Why start now, and why go slowly

- **Fitness declines faster with age.** Peak aerobic capacity falls 3–6% per decade in the 20s and 30s, and more than 20% per
  decade from the 70s on. It falls at about the same rate at every activity level [fleg2005]. Training does not stop the
  slide; it raises the point the slide starts from.
- **There is a line to stay above.** Independent living at 85 needs about 18 mL/kg/min of aerobic power in men
  [paterson1999]. The higher the capacity built at 40–60, the later in life that line is crossed.
- **More fitness kept paying.** Elite against low fitness carried a hazard ratio of 0.20 for death, with "no observed upper
  limit of benefit" [mandsager2018]. Each MET more went with 13% lower risk [kodama2009].
- **Power fades first.** Muscle power declines earlier and more steeply than strength [reid2012], so this plan keeps jumping
  for life.
- **Bones start early.** Men's bone loss began at 25–39 in a Canadian cohort [berger2008].

The urgent part is starting; everything after that is slow on purpose. Sixty years is a long time. What ends a plan like
this is an injury or quitting, not a slow start. So the plan ramps up over 3 weeks, adds load in small steps, and lets a
15-minute day count.

## 2. The tortoise rules

1. **Every day has a session.** A full day is about 60 minutes. When life wins, the 15-minute morning block alone makes a
   **Shell day**, and it keeps the streak (*our rule*).
2. **Never miss twice.** Missing one day happens; two in a row is what to avoid. Do not double up the next day
   (*our rule*).
3. **Heavy for you, never maximal alone.** Use a load you can lift 8–12 times with good form, building to 3 sets [ros2022].
   End each set 1–2 reps before form would break (*our rule*). No one-rep-maximum attempts at home.
4. **Small steps.** Running and rope time grow by at most 10% a week (*our rule*). Novice runners who raised their weekly
   distance by more than 30% over two weeks had more distance-related injuries [nielsen2014].
5. **Every fourth week is light** (a deload week): fewer sets, shorter runs, half the intervals. The morning block and the
   splits continue as usual (*our rule*).
6. **Test every 12 weeks** with the Tortoise Test (§10), and move up by the numbers, not by mood (*our rule*).
7. **Outdoors when the weather allows.** Runs and the long Saturday session are outside in daylight; the rope is the indoor
   fallback (§12).
8. **Respect the pain rules** (§13). Muscle burn is fine; joint or tendon pain means going back a level.

## 3. How hard is hard

| Word | What it means | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Easy** | You can talk in full sentences | The talk test. With a heart-rate monitor: at or below about 70% of HRmax, the "long slow distance" intensity in [helgerud2007] |
| **Hard** (intervals) | Only a few words at a time; you could not hold it much longer than the interval | 90–95% of HRmax by the end of each 4-minute interval [helgerud2007] |
| **HRmax** | Maximal heart rate | About 208 − 0.7 × age [tanaka2001]. At 40: about 180, so easy is up to about 126 and hard is about 162–171. One person's real HRmax can be 10 or more beats off the formula, so the talk test wins when they disagree (*our rule*) |
| **RIR** | Reps in reserve: how many more clean reps you could have done | Strength sets end at RIR 1–2 (*our rule*) |
| **Stretch 1–10** | 10 = the worst stretch pain imaginable | Splits work at 5–7: strong, never sharp (*our rule*) |

A heart-rate monitor is optional; the talk test works everywhere.

## 4. The shape of a day

| Part | When | Time | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning block** | After waking | 15 min | Straight-back routine, wrist prep, handstand practice, balance |
| **Main session** | Lunch or evening | About 45 min (longer on Saturday) | Warm-up, the day's main work, then a split block |
| **Daily habits** | All day | — | Steps, three short vigorous bursts, time outdoors, breaks from sitting |

- **Shell day:** the morning block alone (15 min). It counts toward the streak. The app colours it amber and a full day
  green.
- **Winter run days:** move the main session to lunch, so the run happens in daylight.

## 5. The week

| Day | Morning (15 min) | Main session | Split block | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Morning block | **Strength A:** jumps, squat, push-up, row, back extension, core, calves | Front (10 min) | ~60 min |
| **Tue** | Morning block | **Easy run** 30–40 min outdoors (indoor rope circuit when needed) | Side (10 min) | ~65 min |
| **Wed** | Morning block | **Strength B:** hinge, pike push-up, step-up, pullover, Nordic curl, carry | Front (10 min) | ~60 min |
| **Thu** | Morning block | **Intervals:** pogo and skater hops, then rope intervals (outdoor running in good weather) | Side (10 min) | ~60 min |
| **Fri** | Morning block | **Strength C:** overhead press, Cossack squat, row, push-up, balance, Copenhagen plank | Both, short (10 min) | ~60 min |
| **Sat** | Morning block | **Long outdoor:** long easy run 45–90 min, then playground hangs and pull-ups | Both (10 min) | ~1.5–2 h |
| **Sun** | Morning block | **Light:** flexibility session 30 min, then an easy walk | In the session | ~45 min + walk |

A full week is about 7 hours; the ramp-up weeks are about 4–5.

**The week against the evidence**

| Pillar | This plan in a full week | The benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate aerobic | Easy run 30–40 min + long run 45–90 min + walking ≈ 150–200 min | 150–300 min a week of moderate activity, or 75–150 min of vigorous [bull2020] |
| Vigorous aerobic | 16 min hard in the 4 × 4, plus rope bursts | 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax raised VO2max 7.2% in 8 weeks (done 3 times a week in the trial) [helgerud2007] |
| Strength | 3 sessions of about 30 min | Mortality risk was lowest at about 30–60 min a week; above that the effect was unclear [momma2022]. The extra minutes here serve bone, the skills and the goals |
| Impact | Rope in every warm-up, jumps, running | About 50 moderate impacts on most days [ros2022] |
| Back extension | Light every morning, loaded on Monday | 3–5 reps held 3–5 s, at least twice a week [ros2022] |
| Balance | Every day | Balance and functional exercise cut the rate of falls by 24% [sherrington2019] |
| Stretching | Each split 5 days a week | Static stretching at least 5 days a week [thomas2018] |
| Steps | 8,000–10,000 a day | Under 60, risk kept falling up to about 8,000–10,000 steps a day [paluch2022] |

## 6. The sessions in detail

### 6.1 Morning block — every day, 15 min

| # | Exercise | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cat–camel | 6 slow rounds | Moves each part of the spine before the day starts |
| 2 | Chin tuck | 8 × 3 s hold | Keeps the head over the shoulders; trains the deep neck muscles |
| 3 | Upper-back extension over a chair back | 6 × 3 s | Opens the upper back that sitting closes |
| 4 | Wall angel | 8 slow | Shoulder blades down and back, arms overhead with the ribs down: the handstand shoulder position |
| 5 | Prone chest lift, arms in a W | 5 × 3–5 s hold | The UK consensus dose for the back extensors [ros2022]. Eight years after a back-extension programme, fewer spine bones had fractured: 1.6% against 4.3% [sinaki2002] |
| 6 | Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch | 30 s per side | Undoes sitting; prepares the front split's back leg |
| 7 | Wrist prep | 90 s | Readies the wrists to carry body weight |
| 8 | Handstand practice at your level (§7.4) | 6 min | A skill grows with frequency: little, often, and fresh (*our rule*) |
| 9 | One-leg stance at your level (§7.12) | 30 s per leg | At 51–75, people who could not stand 10 s on one leg died at a 1.84 times higher rate [araujo2022] |
| 10 | Heel-to-toe walk | 10 steps forward, 10 back | Balance in motion; balance work at least twice a week [octftf] |

### 6.2 Warm-up — before every main session, 5 min

| Exercise | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Easy rope (or marching, or jumping jacks) | 2 min | Raises body temperature; a couple of hundred easy impacts covers the "about 50 moderate impacts on most days" [ros2022] |
| World's greatest stretch | 3 per side | Hips, hamstrings and upper back in one movement |
| Leg swings, front–back and side–side | 10 each per leg | Hips ready for running and the splits |
| Scapular push-ups | 10 | Shoulder blades and the serratus muscles |
| Bodyweight squats | 10 | Knees and hips through their full range |

### 6.3 Strength A — Monday, about 30 min after the warm-up

Exercises are done in pairs: alternate A1 and A2, and rest after each pair.

| # | Exercise (ladder) | Sets × reps | Rest | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0 | Jumps (§7.10): broad jump, stick the landing | 4 × 3 | 60 s | Done first, while fresh. Power fades earliest [reid2012]; landings load bone [ros2022] |
| A1 | Squat (§7.1): goblet squat, then Bulgarian split squat, then pistol | 3 × 8–12 | after A2 | Legs carry everything. Putting the whole body on one leg makes a fixed dumbbell heavy enough |
| A2 | Push-up (§7.3): incline, then floor, then archer | 3 × 6–15 | 75 s | In active men, those who could do more than 40 push-ups had far fewer heart events over 10 years than those who could do fewer than 10 [yang2019] |
| B1 | One-arm dumbbell row, hand and knee on a chair | 3 × 8–12 per side | after B2 | The upper-back pull that holds the shoulders back |
| B2 | Back extension (§7.6), 3-s hold at the top | 3 × 8 | 60 s | 3 sets of 8, the arms moving from the sides to a W to the ears [katzman2010] |
| C1 | Core (§7.7): dead bug, then hollow hold | 3 × 8 per side, or 20–40 s | after C2 | Trunk stability; the hollow line is the handstand line |
| C2 | Single-leg calf raise on a step, 3 s down | 2 × 10–15 per leg | 45 s | Calves and Achilles tendons for rope and running |

### 6.4 Strength B — Wednesday

| # | Exercise (ladder) | Sets × reps | Rest | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Hinge (§7.2): dumbbell Romanian deadlift, then single-leg | 3 × 8–12 | after A2 | The hip hinge is the safe way to bend and lift for life [ros2022]; it loads the hamstrings at length, which serves the front split |
| A2 | Vertical push (§7.4, strength track): pike push-up, then feet on a chair, then wall handstand push-up negatives | 3 × 5–10 | 75 s | The pressing strength a handstand stands on |
| B1 | Step-up onto a chair or stair, a dumbbell in each hand | 3 × 8–10 per leg | after B2 | Stairs at 99 |
| B2 | Dumbbell pullover, lying on the floor | 3 × 10–12 | 60 s | Lats and overhead range without a bar |
| C1 | Nordic hamstring curl, heels under a sofa (§7.2) | 3 × 3–6 slow lowerings | after C2 | Programmes that included it cut hamstring injuries by up to 51% [vandyk2019]; strong, long hamstrings for running and the front split |
| C2 | Farmer carry or hold, both dumbbells | 3 × 45–60 s | 60 s | Grip: each 5 kg less grip strength went with a 16% higher death rate [leong2015] |

### 6.5 Strength C — Friday

| # | Exercise (ladder) | Sets × reps | Rest | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Standing dumbbell overhead press | 3 × 6–10 | after A2 | Shoulder strength and the locked-out arms a handstand stands on |
| A2 | Cossack squat, holding one dumbbell at the chest | 3 × 6–8 per side | 75 s | Strength through a full range improved range of motion as much as stretching did [afonso2021]; the side split |
| B1 | Two-arm bent-over row, later renegade row | 3 × 8–10 (renegade 6–8 per side) | after B2 | The second pulling day |
| B2 | Push-up, one variant harder than Monday's, or slower | 2 × 6–12 | 60 s | The second push day |
| C1 | Single-leg reach (bodyweight), slow | 2 × 6 per side | after C2 | Balance and hip control; balance and functional exercise cut falls [sherrington2019] |
| C2 | Copenhagen plank, knee on a chair (§7.7) | 2 × 15–30 s per side | 45 s | Inner-thigh strength for the side split and the groin |
| D | Side plank, then tibialis raise | 2 × 30 s per side; 2 × 15–20 | — | The side of the trunk [octftf]; shins and ankles for running and jumping |

### 6.6 Easy run — Tuesday

- **Session:** 5 min brisk walk, then 30–40 min easy running (talk test). From Block 2, add 4–6 strides of 20 s (fast but
  relaxed, walking back between them). Finish with 3 min of walking.
- **Not yet able to run 30 min comfortably:** use the run–walk ladder (§7.8) instead.
- **Why:** runners had a 30% lower risk of death and about 3 years more life expectancy, and 5–10 slow minutes a day was
  enough [lee2014]. Any running, even once a week, went with 27% lower risk [pedisic2020]. Easy running builds the base
  without the cost of hard running.
- **Indoor fallback** (ice, storm, dangerous footing): 8 × (3 min easy rope, 1 min marching in place), about 32 min.
  Stair walking also works.

### 6.7 Intervals — Thursday

- **Warm-up:** 8 min, made of 4 min easy rope, the warm-up drills (§6.2) and 2 × 20 s of faster rope.
- **Plyometrics, while fresh:** pogo hops 2 × 20, then skater hops 2 × 6 per side.
- **The main set, by block (§8):**

  | Block | Main set |
  |---|---|
  | 1 | 10 × (30 s fast, 60 s easy) |
  | 2 | 6 × (1 min hard, 1 min easy) |
  | 3 | 5 × (2 min hard, 2 min easy) |
  | 4 onward | 4 × (4 min hard, 3 min easy): the 4 × 4 of [helgerud2007] |

- **Where:** indoors on the rope, using the alternate-foot running step and high knees, and later double-unders. Outdoors in
  good weather, run the same intervals, uphill if possible.
- **Cool-down:** 3 min easy.
- **Why:** with total work matched, only the interval groups raised VO2max; 4 × 4 gave +7.2% in 8 weeks [helgerud2007].
  Aerobic fitness is the measure most strongly linked to survival [mandsager2018]. Older adults doing intervals twice a week
  for five years showed a trend to lower mortality [stensvold2020].

### 6.8 Long outdoor — Saturday

- **Long easy run** (or run–walk, or a hike) in daylight. It starts at 30–45 min in Block 1 and grows by at most 10% a week
  (*our rule*), to 60–75 min in year 1 and up to 90 min later.
- **Playground, 15 min after the run (§7.5):** dead hangs 3 × max; the day's pull-up step (scapular pulls, jumping negatives,
  negatives, then pull-ups); hanging knee raises 2 × 8.
- **No bars, or icy bars:** at home, 3 × 10 one-arm dumbbell rows per side and 2 × 60 s farmer holds.
- **Split block:** both splits, 10 min, while warm from the run.

### 6.9 Split blocks — after the main session, 10 min

Hold each position still: static stretching gave larger gains than bouncing or contract–relax methods [thomas2018]. Breathe
slowly and sink a little on each breath out. Stretch at 5–7 out of 10, never sharp pain (*our rule*).

**Front-split block** (Monday, Wednesday; a short version Friday and Saturday; long on Sunday)

| # | Position | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch, pelvis tucked | 45 s per side |
| 2 | Half split: front leg straight, hips over the back knee | 60 s per side |
| 3 | Supported front split: hands on chairs or stacked books, slide down until the stretch is 5–7 | 60 s per side |
| 4 | Active front-leg lift: sit tall, leg straight, lift the heel 5 cm off the floor | 2 × 8 per side |

**Side-split block** (Tuesday, Thursday; a short version Friday and Saturday; long on Sunday)

| # | Position | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frog stretch: knees wide, rock the hips back | 60 s |
| 2 | Horse stance (a wide squat hold) | 45 s |
| 3 | Seated straddle, leaning forward from the hips with a flat back | 60 s |
| 4 | Supported side split: hands on chairs, slide the feet apart | 60 s |
| 5 | Side-lying leg raise | 2 × 8 per side |

**Why:** stretching at least 5 days a week improves range of motion. Weekly time and frequency mattered, not the length of one
session [thomas2018]. Strength through a full range helps as much as stretching [afonso2021], which is why the Cossack squats,
Romanian deadlifts, Nordics and Copenhagen planks are in the strength days.

**What to expect:** in the first 3–8 weeks, most of the gain is the nervous system tolerating the stretch, not longer muscle
[weppler2010]. Real change takes months to years. Hip bones differ in shape, and a flat side split is not possible for every
hip. So progress is measured in centimetres from the floor, and any steady gain counts.

**Sunday flexibility session (30 min):** both split blocks in their long form (each hold 2 × 60 s); a pancake (seated straddle
fold) 2 × 60 s; 90/90 hip switches × 10; a deep squat hold 2 × 60 s; and for the handstand, a shoulder stretch (puppy pose,
hands on a chair) 2 × 45 s.

### 6.10 Daily habits

| Habit | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Steps | 8,000–10,000 a day | [paluch2022] |
| Exercise snacks | 3 bursts a day of 1–2 min vigorous effort: stairs two at a time, a fast uphill walk, 30 fast rope skips | Three such bursts a day went with 38–40% lower mortality in non-exercisers [stamatakis2022] |
| Sitting breaks | Stand and move every 30–60 min at a desk (*our rule*) | The WHO advises less sitting, though no threshold was proven [bull2020] |
| Daylight | Some time outside every day, at midday when possible (*our rule*) | §12 |

## 7. Progression ladders

**When to move up** (*our rule*; the app suggests, you confirm):
- **Reps:** all sets at the top of the range with 1 or more reps in reserve, in two sessions in a row. Then move to the next
  level and start at the bottom of its range.
- **Holds:** all sets at the target time.
- **Endurance:** at most +10% a week.
- **After two or more weeks off:** go one level down for a week.

### 7.1 Squat

| Level | Variant | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Goblet squat (one dumbbell held at the chest) | 3 × 12 |
| 2 | Goblet squat, 3 s down and a 1-s pause | 3 × 10 |
| 3 | Split squat, a dumbbell in each hand | 3 × 12 per leg |
| 4 | Bulgarian split squat (rear foot on a chair), dumbbells | 3 × 12 per leg |
| 5 | Bulgarian split squat, 3 s down, 1.5 reps | 3 × 10 per leg |
| 6 | Pistol to a chair (sit and stand on one leg) | 3 × 8 per leg |
| 7 | Full pistol squat, later holding a dumbbell | 3 × 5 per leg |

### 7.4 Handstand

**Skill track** (morning block)

| Level | Drill | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Wall plank: hands on the floor, feet on the wall at hip height, body in an L | 3 × 40 s |
| H2 | Wall walk: walk the feet up the wall and the hands in, to 60–80° | 3 × 3 controlled walks |
| H3 | Chest-to-wall handstand: body hollow, shoulders pushed tall | 3 × 60 s |
| H4 | Kick-up with your back to the wall, and bail practice (turn a hand and step out sideways, a cartwheel exit) | 10 soft kick-ups; a bail to either side on demand |
| H5 | Heel pulls: from the back-to-wall handstand, ease the heels off and balance | 10 × 5 s balanced |
| H6 | Freestanding kick-ups in open space | 3 holds of 10 s in one session |
| H7 | Freestanding hold | 30 s, then 60 s |
| H8 | Variations: tuck, straddle, and later a press to handstand (it needs the pancake, so the splits help) | — |

**Strength track** (Wednesday A2; the overhead press on Friday, hollow holds on Monday and hangs on Saturday support it)

| Level | Drill | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Pike push-up, feet on the floor | 3 × 10 |
| S2 | Pike push-up, feet on a chair | 3 × 10 |
| S3 | Wall handstand push-up negatives (chest to wall, lower in 5 s) | 3 × 5 |
| S4 | Wall handstand push-up, partial range | 3 × 5 |
| S5 | Wall handstand push-up, full range | 3 × 5 |

**How long:** no trial has measured adults learning a handstand. Coaches commonly report many months to a couple of years
from zero to a steady freestanding hold, with near-daily practice. That is why it gets 6 minutes every morning instead of
one long session a week.

### 7.6 Back extension (posture)

|---|---|---|

## 8. The first 13 weeks

### Week 0: learn and test

- **Saturday and Sunday:** the Tortoise Test (§10).
- **The other days:** the morning block, and each main session at half volume to learn the movements. If running is new,
  walk briskly instead of running.

**Starting levels from the test**

| Family | Rule |
|---|---|
| Push | Maximum push-ups 0–4 → level 1; 5–9 → 2; 10–19 → 3; 20–29 → 4; 30 or more → 5 |
| Run | 30 min of easy running is comfortable → R8; 10 min → R4; less → R1 |
| Rope | 1 min continuous → J2; 3 min → J3; less → J1 |
| Pull | Dead hang under 30 s → P1; 30 s or more and a controlled 5-s negative → P3 |
| Everything else | Level 1 or 2 for the first block, to learn the form; the ladder rules move it up fast |

### Block 1 (Weeks 1–4): ramp

| Week | Changes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strength 2 sets; runs and rope at the starting step; intervals 10 × (30 s, 60 s) at moderate effort; long run 30–45 min; splits at 4–5 out of 10 |
| 2 | Same; splits at 5–6 |
| 3 | Strength 3 sets |
| 4 | Light week: 2 sets, runs 30% shorter, half the intervals |

### Block 2 (Weeks 5–8)

Add strides on Tuesday; intervals 6 × (1 min, 1 min); Saturday up by at most 10% a week. Week 8 is light.

### Block 3 (Weeks 9–12)

Intervals 5 × (2 min, 2 min). Week 12 is light, with the first retest (Tortoise Test #1) on its weekend.

### From Week 13

The 4 × 4 intervals begin, and the 12-week cycle repeats (§9).

## 9. Over the years: cycles and stages to 99

**The cycle** (*our rule*): a block is 4 weeks (3 building, 1 light). Three blocks make 12 weeks, and the Tortoise Test falls
in every third light week. Once a year, the plan itself gets a review: new evidence, and what worked.

| Ages | Focus | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Late 30s to 40** (year 1) | The habit, a safe ramp, learning the skills | Week 0 to the full plan; handstand H1–H4; running up to 60 min |
| **40–49** | Build the reserve: peak aerobic power, strength, skills | Weekly 4 × 4; pistols and pull-ups; a freestanding handstand; splits progress; add backpack load when the dumbbells get light. Measure height once a year from now on: a loss of more than 2 cm between measurements is a reason for a spine X-ray [oc2023] |
| **50–59** | Keep the intensity, protect the joints | The same structure, with more recovery if needed. A bone-density check after a fracture or with two risk factors [oc2023] |
| **60–69** | Protect bone and power | Keep jumps and intervals while the bones allow. Bone density at 65 with one risk factor [oc2023]. After a spine fracture, impact only up to brisk walking [ros2022] |
| **70–84** | Test and treat; balance first | Bone density at 70 for everyone [oc2023]. Keep the intervals [stensvold2020]. Balance daily [sherrington2019]. Practise getting up off the floor every week |
| **85–99** | Keep what you have | The morning block every day, walking and jogging, carries, getting up off the floor, small jumps if safe. Independent living at 85 needs about 18 mL/kg/min [paterson1999] |

## 10. The Tortoise Test

**When:** in Week 0, then every 12 weeks in the light week (§9).
**How:** rested, over two days, in the same conditions each time. The app keeps the results only on the phone.

### Day 1 — outdoors (Saturday)

| Test | How | Record | Aim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rockport walk** | Walk 1 mile (1.61 km) as fast as possible on flat ground. Take the heart rate at the finish (a monitor, or count 15 s and multiply by 4) | Time, heart rate, body weight | The app estimates VO2max with the Rockport equation [kline1987], valid at ages 30–69. Aim: rising over the years and far above 18 mL/kg/min [paterson1999]. A pulse counted after stopping reads a little low, so compare like with like (*our rule*) |
| **Broad jump** | Feet together, swing the arms, jump forward and hold the landing; best of 3 | cm | At least your own height (*our rule*) |
| **Dead hang** | Playground bar, overhand grip, arms straight | Seconds | 120 s (*our rule*); grip predicts survival [leong2015] |
| **Farmer hold** | Both dumbbells, standing tall | Seconds | Rising |

### Day 2 — indoors (Sunday)

| Test | How | Record | Aim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Height** (once a year) | Without shoes, in the morning | cm | Down more than 2 cm since the last measurement means seeing a doctor [oc2023] |
| **Wall test** | Heels, buttocks and back against a wall, eyes level; measure the gap between the back of the head and the wall | cm | 0 cm. Any gap raised the likelihood of a hidden spine fracture 4.6-fold [green2004]; more than 5 cm means seeing a doctor [oc2023]; 6.5 cm or more best marked hyperkyphosis [wiyanad2018] |
| **Sit-to-rise** | From standing, sit down on the floor and stand up again, using as little support as possible. Each way starts at 5 points; subtract 1 for each support used (hand or knee) | 0–10 | 10. At 51–80, each extra point went with 21% better survival [brito2014] |
| **One-leg stance** | Each leg, eyes open (stop at 60 s), then eyes closed (stop at 60 s) | Seconds | Eyes closed 30 s or more (*our rule*). 10 s with eyes open is the floor that predicted survival at 51–75 [araujo2022] |
| **Push-ups** | Strict: chest to fist height, no rest at the top | Maximum reps | 40 or more [yang2019] |
| **Deep squat hold** | Heels down, feet hip-width | Seconds, up to 120 | 120 s (*our rule*) |
| **Splits** | Front split (each side) and side split, hands on supports; measure from the crotch to the floor | cm | 0 cm |
| **Handstand** | Chest-to-wall hold; best freestanding hold | Seconds | 60 s at the wall, then freestanding |
| **Resting heart rate** | In the morning before getting up, count for 60 s | Beats per minute | A falling or steady trend |

## 12. Outdoors, weather and daylight

- **Run outdoors whenever the footing is safe.** In winter, put the run at lunch for daylight.
- **Bad weather** (*our rule*): ice or dangerous footing means the indoor rope version. Cold alone means dressing in layers
  and going.
- **Vitamin D:** winter sun at southern Canadian latitudes makes none. On cloudless days, skin made no previtamin D3 from
  November through February at 42° N, and from October through March at 52° N [webb1988]. Summer midday sun does make it.
  Vitamin D intake is nutrition, which is outside this app; that is a question for a doctor.
- **Daylight still matters in winter:** running outside, fresh air, real ground. The app records outdoor minutes and shows
  sunrise, sunset and the length of the day.
- **Summer** (*our rule*): avoid sunburn, and run early or late in the heat.

## 13. Safety

- **Before starting:** with chest pain, fainting, a heart, lung or metabolic condition, or high blood pressure, see a doctor
  before the hard intervals (*our rule*).
- **Stop at once and get help** with chest pain or pressure, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, or a racing or irregular
  heartbeat.
- **Pain rules** (*our rule*):
  - Muscle burn during a set and soreness the next day are normal.
  - Joint or tendon pain up to 3 out of 10 that settles by the next morning: carry on at the same level.
  - Pain above 3, sharp pain, or pain that is worse the next morning: drop one level.
  - Pain that lasts 2 weeks: see a physiotherapist.
- **Handstands:**
  - Being upside down raises the pressure in the head and eyes. Skip inversions with glaucoma, a retinal problem or
    uncontrolled high blood pressure, and stop with a headache or a change in vision.
  - Practise over a soft floor with clear space around you.
  - Learn to bail (H4) before freestanding work.
- **Splits:** stretch warm muscles only; no bouncing; never into sharp pain.
- **Lifting:** keep the upper back straight and hinge at the hips; avoid deep forward bending under load [ros2022].
- **Impact:** after a spine fracture, impact only up to brisk walking [ros2022].
- **Illness:** with a fever, rest; come back one level down (*our rule*).

## 14. Adapting the plan

| Situation | What to do (*our rule*) |
|---|---|
| A busy day | A Shell day: the morning block only |
| Travel without dumbbells | Squat and hinge ladders use one leg and slow tempo; rows use a filled backpack; run outside; the rope travels |
| A missed week | Resume at the previous block's levels |
| Two or more weeks off | One level down for a week |
| Swapping days | Keep a day between strength sessions; no intervals the day before the long run |
| The full week is too much | Cut in this order: Saturday's run to 45 min; Tuesday's run to a brisk walk; Strength C; split blocks to 3 days each. Never cut the morning block |
| The dumbbells get too light | One-leg and tempo levels first; then a loaded backpack (1 litre of water = 1 kg); adjustable dumbbells are a later option, not needed now |

## Appendix A. Exercise library

Each entry gives how to do it, the key cue, and the most common mistake. The app's library shows the same text.

### Spine and posture

| Exercise | How | Key cue | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat–camel | On hands and knees, round the whole back up, then let it sag gently and look forward | Move slowly, one segment at a time | Moving only the neck |
| Chin tuck | Standing, or lying with the head on the floor: glide the head straight back (a double chin), hold 3 s | Glide back, do not nod | Tipping the chin down |
| Upper-back extension over a chair | Sit tall, hands behind the head, lean back so the top of the chair back presses below the shoulder blades | Ribs down; bend the upper back | Arching the lower back |
| Wall angel | Heels, buttocks, upper back and head against a wall; arms in a W against the wall; slide up to a Y and back | Keep every contact point | Ribs flaring, lower back leaving the wall |

|---|---|---|---|
