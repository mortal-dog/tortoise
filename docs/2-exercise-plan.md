# 2 · Exercise plan

> **The goal:** at 99, stand with a straight back, jump, and run.
> **On the way:** a freestanding handstand, a front split and a side split.

This is the training plan the Tortoise app runs. It needs three things: a yoga mat, a jump rope and one pair of fixed
dumbbells, 7–15 kg each (D-063). The rest is already at home: a wall, a chair, a sofa, a few thick books (supports in
the splits), a stair or a thick book (calf raises). There is no bar. Running happens outdoors whenever the weather
allows; a longer brisk walk around a room replaces it indoors.

**Safe before clever** (*our rule*, D-056): every exercise is one that is hard to get wrong in a way that hurts. A drill
whose usual mistake injures (a rounded back under load, a fall, a hard one-leg or sideways landing, a sprint, a sudden
heavy lowering) was replaced by a safer one that trains the same thing. The handstand and split goals stay, by the
owner's choice; D-061 later cut every drill whose work another one already does.

It is written for a healthy adult starting in their late 30s. It starts gently, with a Week 0 at half volume followed by a 3-week
ramp; there is no fitness test (D-067).

**How to read the numbers.**
- A number taken from evidence names its study or guideline in [sources.md](sources.md), in square brackets like
  `[lee2014]`.
- The doses in the tables (sets, reps, seconds, minutes, rests), the ladder steps and pass targets and the
  milestones are this plan's own choices, *our rule*, unless a source is named beside them.
- In running text, a number that is this plan's own choice is marked *our rule*.

**Not medical advice.** Read [§13 Safety](#13-safety) before starting.

## 1. The goals and what they need

| At 99, and on the way | What it needs | Where it lives in the week |
|---|---|---|
| **A straight back** | Strong back extensors, a mobile upper back, strong bones (a crushed spine bone tips the back forward) | Morning block every day; loaded back extension Monday; bone loading from lifts, jumps, rope and running |
| **Jump** | Leg power, springy tendons, strong bones, confident landings | Squat jumps Monday and Thursday; rope in the strength and interval warm-ups |
| **Run** | An aerobic base and peak aerobic power; feet, calves and tendons used to running | Easy run Tuesday, intervals Thursday, long run Saturday; calf work |
| **Get up off the floor, carry, climb stairs, not fall** | Leg strength, grip, balance | Strength A–C, carries, daily balance |
| **Handstand** | Wrists that carry body weight, shoulder strength, full overhead range, a hollow body line, the balance skill | 6 minutes every morning; pike push-ups, hollow holds |
| **Front split** | Long hamstrings (front leg) and hip flexors (back leg), and strength at that length | Front-split block 5 days a week; bridges and hip thrusts |
| **Side split** | Long, strong adductors (inner thighs), and hips shaped to allow it | Side-split block 5 days a week; wide goblet squats |

### Why a straight back needs strong bones, not only good posture

- **A hunched back is more than looks.** Older people with a hyperkyphotic posture died at a 1.44 times higher rate
  [kado2004]. The most hunched had 3.7 times the odds of needing their arms to rise from a chair [kado2005].
- **Posture work has to last a lifetime.** In adults over 60 with a hunched back, spine strengthening with posture
  training cut the curve by 3.0° in 6 months, and 1.6° of it came back in the 6 months after the programme ended
  [sheaf2017]. That is why the straight-back routine runs every morning rather than in a 12-week course.
- **Bone answers loads that come and go.** Bone responds to dynamic loading, such as lifting, lowering and landing, not to
  a weight held still [turner1998]. In an animal experiment, a bone under a constant load lost as much as an unloaded
  one [lanyon1984]. In women around and after menopause, walking alone did not significantly change spine density
  [ma2013].
- **Heavier, for you, builds more.** These trials were in people with low bone mass. Supervised heavy lifting with
  jumps raised spine density by 2.9% in women past menopause [liftmor2018] and by 4.1% in older men [harding2020]. In
  the same trial the women's upper-back curve fell 6.7°, against 1.6° in controls [liftmor2019]. Leg resistance
  training raised hip density in women past menopause [howe2011], and in men exercise had small, positive effects on
  bone [mages2021]. At home, without supervision, the plan stays at "heavy for you" (8–12 clean reps) [ros2022], made
  heavier by one-leg variants, slower tempo and backpack load.

### Why start now, and why go slowly

- **Fitness declines faster with age.** Peak aerobic capacity falls 3–6% per decade in the 20s and 30s, and more than 20% per
  decade from the 70s on. It falls at about the same rate at every activity level [fleg2005]. Training does not stop the
  slide; it raises the point the slide starts from.
- **There is a line to stay above.** Independent living at 85 needs about 18 mL/kg/min of aerobic power in men and 15
  in women [paterson1999]. The higher the capacity built at 40–60, the later in life that line is crossed.
- **More fitness kept paying.** Elite against low fitness carried a hazard ratio of 0.20 for death, with "no observed upper
  limit of benefit" [mandsager2018]. Each MET more went with 13% lower risk [kodama2009].
- **Power fades first.** Muscle power declines earlier and more steeply than strength [reid2012], so this plan keeps jumping
  for life.
- **Bones start early.** Men's bone loss began at 25–39 in a Canadian cohort [berger2008].

The urgent part is starting; everything after that is slow on purpose. Sixty years is a long time. What ends a plan like
this is an injury or quitting, not a slow start. So the plan ramps up over 3 weeks, adds load in small steps, and lets a
15-minute day count.

## 2. The tortoise rules

1. **Every day has a session.** A full day is about 60 minutes. When life wins, the 15-minute morning block alone makes a
   **Shell day**, and it keeps the streak (*our rule*).
2. **Never miss twice.** Missing one day happens; two in a row is what to avoid. Do not double up the next day
   (*our rule*).
3. **Heavy for you, never maximal alone.** Use a load you can lift 8–12 times with good form, building to 3 sets [ros2022].
   End each set 1–2 reps before form would break (*our rule*). No one-rep-maximum attempts at home.
4. **Small steps.** The run–walk ladder moves one step at a time, with at least a week on each step. Beyond the ladder,
   running time grows by at most 10% a week (*our rule*). Novice runners who raised their weekly
   distance by more than 30% over two weeks seemed more prone to distance-related injuries, though the difference was
   not statistically significant [nielsen2014].
5. **Every fourth week is light** (a deload week): fewer sets, shorter runs, half the intervals. The morning block and the
   splits continue as usual (*our rule*).
6. **Move one level at a time, when you say so** (§7): the app asks every few weeks (*our rule*).
7. **Outdoors when the weather allows.** Runs and the long Saturday session are outside in daylight; a longer brisk walk
   indoors is the indoor version (§12).
8. **Respect the pain rules** (§13). Muscle burn is fine; joint or tendon pain means going back a level.

## 3. How hard is hard

| Word | What it means | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Easy** | You can talk in full sentences | The talk test. With a heart-rate monitor: at or below about 70% of HRmax, the "long slow distance" intensity in [helgerud2007] |
| **Hard** (intervals) | Only a few words at a time; you could not hold it much longer than the interval | 90–95% of HRmax by the end of each 4-minute interval [helgerud2007] |
| **HRmax** | Maximal heart rate | About 208 − 0.7 × age [tanaka2001]. At 40: about 180, so easy is up to about 126 and hard is about 162–171. The formula is an average, and one person's real HRmax can sit well away from it, so the talk test wins when they disagree (*our rule*) |
| **RIR** | Reps in reserve: how many more clean reps you could have done | Strength sets end at RIR 1–2 (*our rule*) |
| **Stretch 1–10** | 10 = the worst stretch pain imaginable | Splits work at 5–7: strong, never sharp (*our rule*) |

A heart-rate monitor is optional; the talk test works everywhere.

## 4. The shape of a day

| Part | When | Time | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning block** | After waking | 15 min | Straight-back routine, wrist prep, handstand practice, balance |
| **Main session** | Lunch to mid-afternoon, 3–4 h after the morning block | About 45 min (50–55 on run and interval days, longer on Saturday) | Warm-up, then the day's main work |
| **Split block** | Right after the main session, in the same sitting | 10 min | Front split, side split, or both in the short form (§6.9) |

- **Shell day:** the morning block alone (15 min). It counts toward the streak. The app colours it amber and a full day
  green.
- **Two sittings a day** (*our rule*): the morning block alone, and later the main session with the split block straight
  after it. Between the main session and the split block there is only a drink and 2 minutes of easy walking, so the
  muscles are still warm for the stretch; a longer break means warming up again first.
- **Why lunch to mid-afternoon** (*our rule*): the 3–4 hours after the morning block rest the wrists and shoulders
  before the day's pressing work, the runs happen in daylight even in winter, and hard work stays well away from bed.
- **Winter run days:** start early in the window, so a long Saturday run ends in daylight.

## 5. The week

| Day | Morning (15 min) | Main session | Split block | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Morning block | **Strength A:** jumps, squat, push-up, row, back extension, core, calves | Front (10 min) | ~60 min |
| **Tue** | Morning block | **Easy run** 30–40 min outdoors (a 45–60 min brisk walk indoors when needed) | Side (10 min) | ~70 min |
| **Wed** | Morning block | **Strength B:** bridge, pike push-up, squat, row, carry | Front (10 min) | ~60 min |
| **Thu** | Morning block | **Intervals:** squat jumps, then rope intervals (outdoor running in good weather) | Side (10 min) | ~55–65 min |
| **Fri** | Morning block | **Strength C:** wide goblet squat, push-up, row, side plank | Both, short form (10 min) | ~60 min |
| **Sat** | Morning block | **Long outdoor:** long easy run 45–90 min (a brisk walk indoors, at most 60 min), then rows and farmer holds at home | Both, short form (10 min) | ~1.5–2 h |
| **Sun** | Morning block | **Light:** an easy walk, then the flexibility session (30 min) | In the session | ~45 min + walk |

A full week is about 7½ hours, and about 8 with the longest Saturday run, plus the Sunday walk. Week 0 is about 6
hours (half volume every day, D-067) and the ramp-up weeks about 6½.

**The week against the evidence**

| Pillar | This plan in a full week | The benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate aerobic | Easy run 30–40 min + long run 45–90 min + the walks ≈ 120–170 min, before the daily steps | 150–300 min a week of moderate activity, or 75–150 min of vigorous, or a mix [bull2020] |
| Vigorous aerobic | 16 min hard in the 4 × 4, plus rope bursts | 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax raised VO2max 7.2% in 8 weeks (done 3 times a week in the trial) [helgerud2007] |
| Strength | 3 sessions of about 30 min | Mortality risk was lowest at about 30–60 min a week; above that the effect was unclear [momma2022]. The extra minutes here serve bone, the skills and the goals |
| Impact | Rope in the strength and interval warm-ups, jumps, running | About 50 moderate impacts on most days [ros2022] |
| Back extension | Light every morning, loaded on Monday | 3–5 reps held 3–5 s, at least twice a week [ros2022] |
| Balance | Every day | Balance and functional exercise cut the rate of falls by 24% [sherrington2019] |
| Stretching | Each split 5 days a week | Static stretching at least 5 days a week [thomas2018] |

## 6. The sessions in detail

### 6.1 Morning block — every day, 15 min

| # | Exercise | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cat–camel | 6 slow rounds | Moves each part of the spine before the day starts |
| 2 | Chin tuck | 8 × 3 s hold | Keeps the head over the shoulders; trains the deep neck muscles |
| 3 | Wall angel | 8 slow | Shoulder blades down and back, arms overhead with the ribs down: the handstand shoulder position |
| 4 | Prone chest lift, arms in a W | 5 × 3–5 s hold | The UK consensus dose for the back extensors [ros2022]. Eight years after a back-extension programme, fewer spine bones had fractured: 1.6% against 4.3% [sinaki2002] |
| 5 | Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch | 30 s per side | Undoes sitting; prepares the front split's back leg |
| 6 | Wrist prep | 90 s | Readies the wrists to carry body weight |
| 7 | Handstand practice at your level (§7.4) | Your level's holds, with rests: 3–6 min in all | A skill grows with frequency: little, often, and fresh (*our rule*) |
| 8 | One-leg stance at your level (§7.12) | The level's time per leg (10–60 s) | At 51–75, people who could not stand 10 s on one leg died at a 1.84 times higher rate [araujo2022] |

### 6.2 Warm-up — before each strength session, 5 min

The run days start with a brisk walk instead (§6.6, §6.8), the interval day has its own longer warm-up (§6.7), and
Sunday's walk warms up the flexibility session.

| Exercise | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Easy rope (or jumping jacks) | 2 min | Raises body temperature; a couple of hundred easy impacts covers the "about 50 moderate impacts on most days" [ros2022] |
| World's greatest stretch | 3 per side | Hips, hamstrings and upper back in one movement |
| Squats with no weight | 10 | Knees and hips through their full range |

### 6.3 Strength A — Monday, about 30 min after the warm-up

Exercises are done in pairs: alternate A1 and A2, and rest after each pair. The tables show each exercise's usual sets
and reps; where it has a ladder, your level's own numbers are in §7.

| # | Exercise (ladder) | Sets × reps | Rest | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0 | Jumps (§7.10): squat jump, stick the landing | 3 × 5 | 60 s | Done first, while fresh. Power fades earliest [reid2012]; landings load bone [ros2022] |
| A1 | Squat (§7.1): goblet squat, then split squat, then Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 8–12 | after A2 | Legs carry everything. Putting the whole body on one leg makes a fixed dumbbell heavy enough |
| A2 | Push-up (§7.3): incline, then floor, then feet on a chair | 3 × 6–15 | 75 s | In active men, those who could do more than 40 push-ups had far fewer heart events over 10 years than those who could do fewer than 10 [yang2019] |
| B1 | Row (§7.5): one-arm dumbbell row, hand and knee on a chair | 3 × 8–12 per side | after B2 | The upper-back pull that holds the shoulders back |
| B2 | Back extension (§7.6), 3-s hold at the top | 3 × 8 | 60 s | 3 sets of 8, the arms moving from the sides to a W to the ears [katzman2010] |
| C1 | Core (§7.7): dead bug, then hollow hold | 3 × 8 per side, or 20–40 s | after C2 | Trunk stability; the hollow line is the handstand line |
| C2 | Single-leg calf raise on a step, 3 s down | 2 × 10–15 per leg | 45 s | Calves and Achilles tendons for rope and running |

### 6.4 Strength B — Wednesday

About 30 min after the warm-up, in pairs as on Monday.

| # | Exercise (ladder) | Sets × reps | Rest | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Bridge (§7.2): glute bridge, then single-leg, then hip thrust | 3 × 8–12 | after A2 | The hips and the back of the legs, lifting with no forward bend of the back under load [ros2022] |
| A2 | Vertical push (§7.4, strength track): pike push-up, then feet on a chair, then slower | 3 × 5–10 | 75 s | The pressing strength a handstand stands on |
| B1 | Squat (§7.1): the second leg day, at your level | 3 × 8–12 | after B2 | Legs twice a week: getting up, carrying and climbing stairs for life |
| B2 | Row (§7.5): one-arm dumbbell row, hand and knee on a chair | 3 × 8–12 per side | 60 s | The second pulling day |
| C | Farmer carry or hold, both dumbbells | 3 × 45–60 s | 60 s | Grip: each 5 kg less grip strength went with a 16% higher death rate [leong2015] |

### 6.5 Strength C — Friday

About 30 min after the warm-up, in pairs as on Monday.

| # | Exercise (ladder) | Sets × reps | Rest | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Wide goblet squat (sumo squat): feet wide, toes out, one dumbbell held at the chest | 3 × 8–12 | after A2 | Strength training improved range of motion as much as stretching did [afonso2021]; the wide stance works the inner thighs of the side split |
| A2 | Push-up, one variant harder than Monday's, or slower | 3 × 6–12 | 75 s | The second push day |
| B1 | Row (§7.5): one-arm dumbbell row, hand and knee on a chair | 3 × 8–12 per side | after B2 | The third pulling day |
| B2 | Side plank (§7.7) | 3 × 20–45 s per side | 60 s | The side of the trunk [octftf] |

### 6.6 Easy run — Tuesday

- **Session:** 5 min brisk walk, then 30–40 min easy running (talk test), then 3 min of walking.
- **Not yet able to run 30 min comfortably:** use the run–walk ladder (§7.8) instead.
- **Why:** runners had a 30% lower risk of death and about 3 years more life expectancy, and 5–10 slow minutes a day was
  enough [lee2014]. Any running, even once a week, went with 27% lower risk [pedisic2020]. Easy running builds the base
  without the cost of hard running.
- **Indoor version** (rain, ice, storm, dangerous footing): 45–60 min of brisk walking indoors (talk test): walk briskly
  in circles around a room, longer than the run because walking is easier (*our rule*). Stairs were considered and
  left out: walking down many flights is where the knees take the most load (D-056).

### 6.7 Intervals — Thursday

- **Warm-up:** 8 min, made of 4 min easy rope, the warm-up drills (§6.2) and 2 × 20 s of faster rope.
- **Plyometrics, while fresh:** squat jumps 2 × 5, landing softly on both feet.
- **The main set, by block (§8):**

  | Block | Main set |
  |---|---|
  | 1 | 10 × (30 s fast, 60 s easy) |
  | 2 | 6 × (1 min hard, 1 min easy) |
  | 3 | 5 × (2 min hard, 2 min easy) |
  | 4 onward | 4 × (4 min hard, 3 min easy): the 4 × 4 of [helgerud2007] |

- **Where:** indoors on the rope, using the alternate-foot running step and high knees. Outdoors in good weather, run the
  same intervals, uphill if possible. The easy parts are an easy bounce or a walk.
- **Cool-down:** 3 min easy.
- **Why:** with total work matched, the interval groups raised VO2max more than steady running did; the 4 × 4 gave
  +7.2% in 8 weeks [helgerud2007]. Low aerobic fitness carried as much risk of death as smoking, diabetes or coronary
  disease, or more [mandsager2018]. Older adults doing intervals twice a week for five years showed a trend to lower
  mortality, not a proven effect [stensvold2020].

### 6.8 Long outdoor — Saturday

- **Long easy run** (or run–walk, or a hike) in daylight, after 5 min of brisk walking. It starts at 30–45 min in
  Block 1 and grows by at most 10% a week (*our rule*), to 60–75 min in year 1 and up to 90 min later.
- **At home, 10 min after the run (or the walk):** 3 × 10 one-arm dumbbell rows per side and 2 × 60 s farmer holds.
- **Indoor version** (rain, ice, storm, dangerous footing): 45–60 min of brisk walking indoors, as on Tuesday (§6.6), then
  the rows and farmer holds. At most 60 min; the long run waits for the next safe Saturday (*our rule*).
- **Split block:** both splits in the short form (§6.9), 10 min, while warm from the run.

### 6.9 Split blocks — after the main session, 10 min

Hold each position still: static stretching gave larger gains than bouncing or contract–relax methods [thomas2018]. Breathe
slowly and sink a little on each breath out. Stretch at 5–7 out of 10, never sharp pain (*our rule*).

**Front-split block** (Monday and Wednesday; in the short form on Friday and Saturday; in the long form on Sunday)

| # | Position | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch, pelvis tucked | 45 s per side |
| 2 | Half split: front leg straight, hips over the back knee | 90 s per side |
| 3 | Supported front split: hands on chairs or stacked books, slide down until the stretch is 5–7 | 90 s per side |

**Side-split block** (Tuesday and Thursday; in the short form on Friday and Saturday; in the long form on Sunday)

| # | Position | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Seated straddle, leaning forward from the hips with a flat back | 2 × 90 s |
| 2 | Supported side split: hands on chairs, slide the feet apart | 2 × 90 s |

Each block keeps the two or three positions that stretch the split itself, held longer (D-061): weekly time and
frequency are what count [thomas2018], so fewer positions held longer give the same stretch time.

**Short form, both splits in 10 min** (Friday and Saturday): the half split and the supported front split, 60 s per
side each; then the seated straddle and the supported side split, 90 s each.

**Long form** (Sunday): every hold of both blocks done twice.

**Why:** static stretching at least 5 days a week was the pattern that improved range of motion. Weekly time and
frequency mattered, not the length of one session [thomas2018]. Strength training improved range of motion as much as
stretching did [afonso2021], which is why the wide goblet squats, bridges and hip thrusts are in the strength days.

**What to expect:** in the first 3–8 weeks, most of the gain is the nervous system tolerating the stretch, not longer muscle.
What longer programmes change in the muscle had not been measured [weppler2010]. Expect the splits to take months to
years, not weeks. Hip bones differ in shape, and a flat side split is not possible for every hip, so any steady
gain counts.

**Sunday flexibility session (30 min), after the easy walk:** both split blocks in their long form (the seated
straddle is the pancake), and a deep squat hold 2 × 60 s.

## 7. Progression ladders

**When to move** (*our rule*; D-069). In a session the dose is fixed: nothing is counted or adjusted. After a session,
for each ladder whose turn has come, the app asks: **one level harder, keep, or one level easier?** You decide; the app
never moves a level by itself.
- **How often:** strength ladders every 2 weeks; the handstand skill track and balance every 3 weeks; the run–walk every
  week; jumps every 4 weeks (bone and tendon adapt slowly). The first check-ins come two weeks or more into Week 1.
- **After "easier":** the next check-in comes after a week, from the lower level, so a recovery can step back up soon.
- **After two weeks or more without a ladder:** it is asked about at once, with "easier" first.
- **Answering:** "harder" when the level's pass target (the table's last column) felt easy with 1 or more reps in reserve
  and nothing hurt the next day; "easier" when it hurt or could not be finished. A level's range ends at its pass
  number and starts about a third below it: a pass at 3 × 12 means working from 3 × 8.
- **Endurance:** one step at a time; beyond the ladders, at most +10% a week.
- **Starting higher:** the start questions set the first levels (§8).

|---|---|---|

| 2 | Goblet squat, 3 s down and a 1-s pause | 3 × 10 |

### 7.4 Handstand

**Skill track** (morning block)

| Level | Drill | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Plank on the hands: arms straight, body in one line, push the floor away | 3 × 20 s |
| H2 | Pike hold: hips high, head between the arms | 3 × 30 s |
| H3 | Pike hold, feet on a chair | 3 × 30 s |
| H4 | Wall plank, feet on the wall at knee height | 3 × 20 s |
| H5 | Wall plank, feet on the wall at thigh height | 3 × 30 s |
| H6 | Wall plank: feet on the wall at hip height, body in an L | 3 × 40 s |
| H7 | Wall walk: walk the feet up the wall and the hands in, to 60–80° | 3 × 3 controlled walks |
| H8 | Chest-to-wall handstand: body hollow, shoulders pushed tall | 3 × 30 s |
| H9 | Chest-to-wall handstand, longer | 3 × 60 s |
| H10 | Kick-up with your back to the wall, and bail practice (turn a hand and step out sideways, a cartwheel exit) | 10 soft kick-ups; a bail to either side on demand |
| H11 | Heel pulls: from the back-to-wall handstand, ease the heels off and balance | 10 × 5 s balanced |
| H12 | Freestanding kick-ups in open space | 3 holds of 10 s in one session |
| H13 | Freestanding hold | 30 s, then 60 s |
| H14 | Variations: tuck, straddle, and later a press to handstand (it needs the pancake, so the splits help) | — |

**Strength track** (Wednesday A2; hollow holds on Monday support it)

| Level | Drill | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Pike push-up, feet on the floor | 3 × 10 |
| S2 | Pike push-up, feet on a chair | 3 × 10 |
| S3 | Pike push-up, feet on a chair, 3 s down | 3 × 10 |

No wall handstand push-ups (D-061): lowering the head to the floor upside down loads the neck, and the pike push-ups
build the same pressing strength.

**How long:** no trial has measured adults learning a handstand. Coaches commonly report many months to a couple of years from zero to a steady freestanding hold, with near-daily practice. That is why it gets a few minutes every morning instead of one long session a week. The first six levels stay on the floor or low on the wall, so a beginner starts where the shoulders and wrists can carry the weight. The time in the morning block is a limit, not a hold: a level asks for 3 holds of 20–60 s with 30 s of rest between them, 3–6 min in all. Move up only when all the holds are done with a straight body and the next morning feels fine. Wrists or shoulders that hurt mean going back a level.

### 7.5 Row

There is no bar, so every pull is a one-arm dumbbell row with one hand and one knee on a chair: the support keeps the
back flat (D-056).

| Level | Drill | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | One-arm row, hand and knee on a chair | 3 × 12 per side |
| P2 | One-arm row, 3 s down | 3 × 12 per side |
| P3 | One-arm row, a 2-s squeeze at the top and 3 s down | 3 × 10 per side |

### 7.6 Back extension (posture)

| Level | Variant | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prone chest lift, arms by the sides | 3 × 8, 3-s hold |
| 2 | Arms in a W | 3 × 8 |
| 3 | Hands by the ears | 3 × 8 |
| 4 | Arms overhead in a Y | 3 × 8 |
| 5 | A loaded backpack on the upper back, starting at 2 kg | 3 × 8; add 1 kg when easy |

Every level keeps the 3-s hold at the top. The levels follow the published protocols: arms from the sides to a W to
the ears in 3 × 8, or a backpack loaded up to 30% of a one-repetition maximum [katzman2010].

### 7.7 Core

| Level | Variant | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dead bug | 3 × 8 per side |
| 2 | Hollow hold, knees bent | 3 × 30 s |
| 3 | Hollow hold, legs straight | 3 × 40 s |

- **Side plank:** 2 × 20 s, building to 45 s, then lift the top leg.

### 7.8 Running: the run–walk ladder

Stay at least a week on each step, and move up when the step felt easy and nothing hurt the next morning (*our rule*).

| Step | Session |
|---|---|
| R1 | 8 × (1 min run, 2 min walk) |
| R2 | 6 × (2 min run, 2 min walk) |
| R3 | 5 × (3 min run, 90 s walk) |
| R4 | 4 × (5 min run, 1 min walk) |
| R5 | 3 × (8 min run, 1 min walk) |
| R6 | 2 × (12 min run, 1 min walk) |
| R7 | 25 min continuous |
| R8 | 30 min continuous. From here Tuesday is 30–40 min, and Saturday grows by at most 10% a week |

### 7.9 Rope

The rope warms up the strength and interval days and carries the indoor intervals (§6.7). Skills, in order: basic
bounce, alternate-foot step, high knees, side swing. No double-unders or criss-cross: a missed double-under whips the
shins and strains the calves, and a criss-cross trips (D-056).

### 7.10 Jumps

| Level | Drill | Pass at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squat jump, stick the landing | 3 × 5 |
| 2 | Squat jump, stick the landing | 4 × 6 |
| 3 | Squat jumps in a row, soft and quiet landings | 4 × 5 |

- Jumps are done fresh, at the numbers shown, from both feet and landing on both feet. A level is passed when every
  landing in a session is soft and controlled.
- Jumping far, on one leg or sideways is left out (D-056): those landings are where knees and ankles get hurt.

### 7.13 Splits

The splits have no levels. Progress in the supported split itself: hold it for 60 s, later 2 × 60 s, and lower the supports (thick books, then thinner,
then none).

## 8. The first 13 weeks

### Week 0: learn

- **Monday to Sunday:** the morning block, and each main session at half volume, to learn the movements. Half volume
  means half the sets (rounded up), half the run or rope minutes and half the interval repeats, all at an easy effort,
  with every ladder at level 1 (*our rule*). If running is new, walk briskly instead of running.

**Starting levels from the start questions**

Five questions are asked when the plan starts (D-069); each answer starts a ladder where its pass is not yet met.

| Question | Rule |
|---|---|
| Floor push-ups in a row | 0–4 → Push 1; 5–14 → 2; 15–29 → 3; 30 or more → 4 |
| An easy run without stopping | Not yet → R1; about 10 min → R4; 30 min or more → R8 |
| One leg for 30 s, eyes open, each leg | Yes → Ba2 |
| A chest-to-wall handstand for 30 s | Yes → H8 |
| Lifting weights at least twice a week for 3 months or more | Yes → Squat 2, Bridge 2, Row P2, Back extension 2 |
| Everything else | Level 1, to learn the form; the check-ins move it (§7) |

### Block 1 (Weeks 1–4): ramp

| Week | Changes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strength 2 sets; runs and rope at the starting step; intervals 10 × (30 s, 60 s) at moderate effort; long run 30–45 min; splits at 4–5 out of 10 |
| 2 | Same; splits at 5–6 |
| 3 | Strength 3 sets; intervals at full effort; splits at 5–7 from here on |
| 4 | Light week: 2 sets, runs 30% shorter, half the intervals |

### Block 2 (Weeks 5–8)

Intervals 6 × (1 min, 1 min); Saturday up by at most 10% a week. Week 8 is light.

### Block 3 (Weeks 9–12)

Intervals 5 × (2 min, 2 min). Week 12 is light.

### From Week 13

The 4 × 4 intervals begin, and the 12-week cycle repeats (§9).

## 9. Over the years: cycles and stages to 99

**The cycle** (*our rule*): a block is 4 weeks (3 building, 1 light). Three blocks make 12 weeks. Once a year, the plan itself gets a review: new evidence, and what worked.

| Ages | Focus | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Late 30s to 40** (year 1) | The habit, a safe ramp, learning the skills | Week 0 to the full plan; handstand H1–H10; the long run up to 60–75 min |
| **40–49** | Build the reserve: peak aerobic power, strength, skills | Weekly 4 × 4; the top squat, bridge and push-up levels; a freestanding handstand; splits progress; add backpack load when the dumbbells get light. |
| **50–59** | Keep the intensity, protect the joints | The same structure, with more recovery if needed. A bone-density check after a fracture or with two risk factors [oc2023] |
| **60–69** | Protect bone and power | Keep jumps and intervals while the bones allow. Bone density at 65 with one risk factor [oc2023]. After a spine fracture, impact only up to brisk walking [ros2022] |
| **70–84** | Test and treat; balance first | Bone density at 70 for everyone [oc2023]. Keep the intervals [stensvold2020]. Balance daily [sherrington2019]. Practise getting up off the floor every week |
| **85–99** | Keep what you have | The morning block every day, walking and jogging, carries, getting up off the floor, small jumps if safe. Independent living at 85 needs about 18 mL/kg/min in men and 15 in women [paterson1999] |

## 12. Outdoors, weather and daylight

- **Run outdoors whenever the footing is safe.** In winter, put the run at lunch for daylight.
- **Bad weather** (*our rule*): rain, ice or dangerous footing means the indoor version, a longer brisk walk around a room (§6.6, §6.8).
  Cold alone means dressing in layers and going.
- **Vitamin D:** winter sun at southern Canadian latitudes makes none. On cloudless days, skin made no previtamin D3 from
  November through February at 42° N, and from October through March at 52° N [webb1988]. In the rest of the year the
  sun does make it. Vitamin D intake is nutrition, which is outside this app; that is a question for a doctor.
- **Daylight still matters in winter:** running outside, fresh air, real ground.
- **Summer** (*our rule*): avoid sunburn, and run early or late in the heat.

## 13. Safety

- **Before starting:** with chest pain, fainting, a heart, lung or metabolic condition, or high blood pressure, see a doctor
  before the hard intervals (*our rule*).
- **Stop at once and get help** with chest pain or pressure, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, or a racing or irregular
  heartbeat.
- **Pain rules** (*our rule*):
  - Muscle burn during a set and soreness the next day are normal.
  - Joint or tendon pain up to 3 out of 10 that settles by the next morning: carry on at the same level.
  - Pain above 3, sharp pain, or pain that is worse the next morning: drop one level.
  - Pain that lasts 2 weeks: see a physiotherapist.
- **Handstands:**
  - Being upside down raises the pressure in the head and eyes. Skip inversions with glaucoma, a retinal problem or
    uncontrolled high blood pressure, and stop with a headache or a change in vision.
  - Practise over a soft floor with clear space around you.
  - Learn to bail (H10) before freestanding work.
- **Splits:** stretch warm muscles only; no bouncing; never into sharp pain.
- **Lifting:** keep the upper back straight; avoid deep forward bending under load [ros2022]. The plan has no lift that
  bends forward under load.
- **Impact:** after a spine fracture, impact only up to brisk walking [ros2022].
- **Illness:** with a fever, rest; come back one level down (*our rule*).

## 14. Adapting the plan

| Situation | What to do (*our rule*) |
|---|---|
| A busy day | A Shell day: the morning block only |
| Travel without dumbbells | Squat and bridge ladders use one leg and slow tempo; rows use a filled backpack; run outside; the rope travels |
| A missed week | Resume at the same levels, starting each range at its bottom |
| Two or more weeks off | One level down for a week |
| Swapping days | Keep a day between strength sessions; no intervals the day before the long run |
| The full week is too much | Cut in this order: Saturday's run to 45 min; Tuesday's run to a brisk walk; Strength C; split blocks to 3 days each. Never cut the morning block |
| The dumbbells get too light | One-leg and tempo levels first; then a loaded backpack (1 litre of water = 1 kg); adjustable dumbbells are a later option, not needed now |
